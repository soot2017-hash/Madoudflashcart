WORD: load
SOURCE: Oxford Learner's Dictionary (dictionary-crawler)

IDIOM: get a load of somebody/something
HEADWORD: load
PART OF SPEECH: noun
PRONUNCIATION: /ləʊd/ | /ləʊd/
SENSE — something that is being carried (usually in large amounts) by a person, vehicle, etc. [cefr: B2] [grammar: countable]
collocation: adjective — heavy, light, full, …
collocation: verb + load — bear, carry, shoulder, …
collocation: preposition — under its, etc. load
example: The trucks waited at the warehouse to pick up their loads.
example: The women came down the hill with their loads of firewood.
example: These backpacks are designed to carry a heavy load.
SENSE — the total amount of something that something can carry or contain [cefr: B2] [grammar: countable]
collocation: adjective — heavy, light, full, …
collocation: verb + load — bear, carry, shoulder, …
collocation: preposition — under its, etc. load
example: a busload of tourists
example: They ordered three truckloads of sand.
example: He put half a load of washing in the machine.
SENSE — the amount of weight that is pressing down on something [grammar: countable] [grammar: usually singular]
collocation: adjective — heavy, light, full, …
collocation: verb + load — bear, carry, shoulder, …
collocation: preposition — under its, etc. load
example: a load-bearing wall
example: Modern backpacks spread the load over a wider area.
example: The table creaked under its heavy load.
SENSE — a large number or amount of people or things; plenty [cefr: B2] [grammar: singular]
example: She's got loads of friends.
example: He wrote loads and loads of letters to people.
example: Uncle Jim brought a whole load of presents for the kids.
SENSE — used to emphasize that something is wrong, stupid, bad, etc. [grammar: singular] [label: load of rubbish, garbage, nonsense, etc.]
example: You're talking a load of rubbish.
SENSE — an amount of work that a person or machine has to do [grammar: countable]
example: Teaching loads have increased in all types of school.
example: Extra warmth from sunlight can put an additional load on the air-conditioning system.
example: We're trying to spread the load by employing more staff.
SENSE — a feeling of responsibility or worry that is difficult to deal with [grammar: countable] [grammar: usually singular]
synonym: burden
example: She thought she would not be able to bear the load of bringing up her family alone.
example: Knowing that they had arrived safely took a load off my mind.
SENSE — the amount of electrical power that is being supplied at a particular time [grammar: countable]
